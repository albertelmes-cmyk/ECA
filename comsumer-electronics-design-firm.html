Problems We Solve

(03)

Why consumer hardware products fail.

The same issues come up repeatedly across consumer hardware projects. Most are avoidable — if they're treated as engineering constraints rather than surprises at the end of development.

Issue 01

Battery life falls short of spec

Peripheral leakage, poorly sequenced power rails, and sleep modes that don't fully engage eat into runtime before firmware ever runs. We measure and budget power at the hardware level, so there are no surprises when the product is in the field.

Issue 02

Unstable Bluetooth or Wi-Fi performance

Range is acceptable on a benchtop but drops when the product is held, enclosed, or used around other devices. Antenna placement, ground plane geometry, and enclosure material all matter. We validate RF performance in-situ — with the product assembled as it will ship.

Issue 03

Unit cost too high to margin properly

Overspecified microcontrollers, redundant power stages, and unnecessary component count inflate BOM cost without adding product value. We challenge every line of the BOM against the real requirements — often finding significant cost reduction without any functional compromise.

Issue 04

Prototypes that don't scale to production

Prototype bring-up works. First production batch has 30% yield loss. The design was never reviewed for assembly — fine-pitch parts with no thermal relief, hand-soldered bodges that crept into the Gerbers, or pads sized for prototyping rather than reflow. We close this gap before the first board is ordered.

Issue 05

EMC pre-compliance failures

Failing pre-compliance late in development is expensive — every board spin costs time and tooling. Radiated emissions problems trace back to layout decisions made early: decoupling strategy, trace routing, and return path continuity. We design for compliance from the first revision.

Issue 06

Over-engineered hardware for the use case

Application processors where a microcontroller would do. External flash for a dataset that fits in internal SRAM. Four power rails when two would work. Complexity adds failure modes, assembly cost, and development time. We right-size the architecture to the actual product requirements.

Case Study

(05)
Project — Consumer Focus Timer

Weeks of runtime. Minimal component count. Volume-ready from day one.

The brief was a compact focus timer — a consumer product that visualises elapsed time through a circular RGB LED array. The product needed to be manufacturable at low cost, run for weeks on a small cell battery, and support Bluetooth LE for configuration. The initial architecture assumptions put the BOM significantly over target and the runtime well short of the required weeks of operation.

We rebuilt the architecture around a low-power microcontroller with aggressive sleep scheduling, replaced the original LED driver with a more efficient integrated solution, and reduced the passive component count substantially through careful schematic review. The RF layout was validated for Bluetooth LE 5.1 performance with the circular LED array directly adjacent to the antenna — a proximity that required careful ground plane management to avoid desensitisation.

The final hardware was designed for high-volume SMT assembly with a component count and placement strategy that kept assembly cost per unit within the original target.

Result

BOM cost reduced to within commercial target. Battery runtime validated at multiple weeks of typical use. Bluetooth LE range confirmed in-product with LED array energised. Assembly validated at volume with high yield — no board spins required post-prototype.
