DFM Services

What design for manufacture actually involves.

DFM is not a checklist applied at the end of a project. It's a set of constraints that belong in every decision from stackup definition through component selection and final placement — before the first Gerber is ever sent to a fab.

01

DFM Review & Risk Assessment

We systematically review existing designs against fabrication and assembly constraints — identifying copper clearances outside fab capability, annular rings at risk under drill tolerance, paste apertures too small for reliable print, and thermal relief decisions that will cause tombstoning or cold joints at volume. Every finding is documented with severity and a specific corrective action.

02

BOM & Cost Optimisation

A BOM that works on a prototype does not automatically work in production. We audit component availability against live distributor stock, flag sole-sourced parts with no approved alternates, and identify opportunities to consolidate values or move to more available packages — reducing cost and supply chain risk simultaneously without changing any electrical function.

Availability auditAlternate sourcingValue consolidationCost reduction
03

Stackup & Fabrication Planning

Layer count, dielectric selection, copper weight, and controlled impedance specifications must be aligned to what your target fabricator can reliably produce at your volume and price point. We define the stackup in consultation with fabrication, validate impedance calculations, specify surface finish against the solderability and shelf-life requirements of your assembly window, and set tolerance classes that don't rely on exceptional fab capability.

Stackup definitionImpedance controlSurface finishFab alignment
04

Assembly Optimisation

Component placement drives assembly cost and yield more than almost any other design decision. We review and correct orientation consistency for feeders, ensure adequate courtyard clearance for pick-and-place, validate fiducial coverage for optical alignment, and check that all SMT components are accessible on a single reflow pass where possible. Through-hole and mixed-technology boards get a separate wave or selective solder assessment.

Problems We Solve

(03)

Where PCB designs break down in production.

The same manufacturing failures appear across different products and different teams. Most of them are traceable to specific design decisions made without manufacturing in mind — and most are avoidable if the right constraints are applied early enough.

Issue 01

Assembly yield drops at volume

A design that assembles cleanly at 50 units starts producing defects at 2,000. Tombstoning on 0402s with asymmetric thermal loads. Solder bridges on fine-pitch QFNs where paste apertures were never reviewed. These are layout problems, not process problems — and they're fixable before the first production order.

Issue 02

Fabrication costs are higher than expected

Tight clearances that push the design into a premium capability tier. Controlled impedance specified on a layer that the chosen fab handles poorly. Surface finish selected for aesthetics rather than solderability. Each decision adds cost. We identify and remove the unnecessary ones without touching electrical performance.

Issue 03

Components go out of stock mid-production

A sole-sourced MCU on a 52-week lead time. A passive that only one distributor carries. A connector family that was discontinued six months ago and nobody noticed until the CM asked. A BOM audit before committing to production finds these before they become a line-stop.

Issue 04

Prototypes worked — production boards don't

Hand-assembled prototypes tolerate a lot. Production SMT reflow does not. Paste deposits that were applied manually now need to print consistently through a stencil at 0.12 mm aperture. Bodge wires that were never removed from the Gerbers. Design intent that wasn't communicated to the CM. The transition from prototype to production exposes everything that was deferred.

Issue 05

No test coverage at production

A board with no test points, no boundary scan access, and no production test procedure ships into a box and fails in the field. Functional failures that would have been caught by a 30-second flying probe test become warranty returns. We design test access in from the start — not as an afterthought when the CM quotes flying probe at £4 per board.

Issue 06

CM feedback creates expensive respins

The CM returns a DFM report on day one of production quoting. It lists 23 issues. Twelve of them require layout changes. Three require component substitutions. The production schedule slips by eight weeks while the design is revised. A thorough DFM pass before sending files to the CM eliminates this entirely.

Case Study

(05)
Project — Consumer Focus Timer

High-volume consumer hardware. One prototype iteration. No CM respins.

The focus timer was designed for high-volume consumer manufacture from the outset — a circular RGB LED array driven by an integrated LED controller, a low-power microcontroller, and a Bluetooth LE radio, all assembled in a compact two-layer form factor with a unit cost target that left minimal margin for BOM overrun or assembly yield loss.

DFM constraints drove placement decisions throughout. All SMT components were oriented consistently for single-pass reflow. The LED array was laid out with matched thermal loads on each pad to prevent tombstoning during reflow — a risk that appeared in the first placement draft and was corrected before the board was sent to fab. Passive values were consolidated across the design to reduce unique line items and simplify kitting. Component availability was checked at target volume before the BOM was locked.

The Bluetooth antenna proximity to the LED array required careful ground plane management — validated during prototype bring-up to confirm no desensitisation under full LED load. The result was a design the CM could quote, build, and test without modification.

Result

BOM cost within target at production volume. Assembly yield high from first production run — no layout-driven defects. Zero CM-requested design changes. Bluetooth LE performance confirmed unaffected by LED load. Production-ready in a single prototype iteration.
