Problems We Solve

(03)

Where commercial electronics projects actually fail.

Most hardware failures are predictable. They come from the same set of shortcuts taken early in the design process. We've seen them all — and we know how to design around them.

Issue 01

EMC failures at pre-compliance

Boards that radiate because decoupling wasn't properly placed, return paths weren't considered, or high-frequency signals were routed like DC. EMC is not something you fix at the end — it's baked into every layer stackup decision, trace route, and ground pour from the start.

Issue 02

RF designs that don't work in the field

Antenna placement that kills range. Ground plane geometry that shifts resonance. Enclosures that attenuate 6 dB of your link budget before the product leaves the building. Wireless performance must be measured in the final form factor — not assumed from a datasheet.

Issue 03

Designs that can't be assembled reliably

Fine-pitch components with no thermal relief. Pads that tombstone under reflow. Copper pour that causes solder bridging at volume. A design that passes prototype bring-up at low quantities often fails completely when assembly lines encounter real-world variance at scale.

Issue 04

Cost overruns from late-stage redesigns

The bill for ignoring manufacturability is always paid eventually — usually during a spin 2 or 3. We eliminate this by treating DFM, BOM cost, and component lead times as hard constraints from day one, not as post-design review comments.

Case Study

(05)
Project — IoT Agricultural Monitoring

Long-range sensing at a five-year deployment horizon.

The brief was to monitor tree health across large agricultural sites using vibration sensing — detecting changes in trunk resonance as a proxy for structural integrity and disease onset. The hardware needed to run for five years on a single battery pack, survive outdoor installation, and transmit reliably across distances beyond the range of conventional wireless protocols.

We designed a multi-layer PCB integrating a low-power microcontroller, precision accelerometer, and LoRa radio module. The RF layout was carefully optimised to maximise transmit efficiency within the regulatory power limit, with the antenna positioned and tuned for performance in proximity to the wooden mounting substrate. Aggressive sleep scheduling in hardware and firmware achieved sub-10 µA average current draw — validating the five-year runtime target on a standard LiSOCl₂ cell.

Result

Production-ready hardware validated at range against gateway, achieving 4.2 km line-of-sight in field testing. First deployment units installed on schedule. Five-year runtime confirmed by power modelling against measured consumption figures.
